[Sanzo Wada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanzo_Wada) (1883–1967) was a
Japanese artist, teacher, and designer who founded the Japan Standard
Color Association (now the Japan Color Research Institute) in 1927.
During the 1930s he published *A Dictionary of Color Combinations*, a
six-volume work collecting 348 curated colour combinations of two to
four named colours each, meant to be used together.

A named, curated combination is a stronger starting point for a palette
than picking colours individually and checking they don't clash — Wada
judged the harmony once, by eye, across hundreds of combinations. That's
a useful complement to algorithmically-generated palettes
(complementary/triadic/etc.): those guarantee a mathematical relationship
between hues, Wada's set guarantees combinations a working designer
judged pleasing.

## Interactive dictionaries

- **[colors.elwyn.co](https://colors.elwyn.co)** (ben elwyn) — browse all
  348 combinations, filter by hex, follow links between related ones.
- **Pete's fork**: [petersgiles/color-combinations-sanzo-wada-public](https://github.com/petersgiles/color-combinations-sanzo-wada-public)
- **[sanzo-wada.dmbk.io](https://sanzo-wada.dmbk.io)** (Dain M. Blodorn
  Kim) — the original interactive browser that inspired the one above.
- **[mattdesl/dictionary-of-colour-combinations](https://github.com/mattdesl/dictionary-of-colour-combinations)**
  — the underlying dataset (names, hex/RGB/CMYK/Lab, which combination
  numbers each colour appears in) that all of the above are built from.
